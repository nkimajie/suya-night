# Suya Night: voice recording script

68 short lines for a **female** voice (F sections) and 112 for a **male** voice (M sections).
Record in round order and stop wherever you run out of time: any line that is not recorded yet
just plays the soft murmur it plays today. The speech bubble always shows the written line.

## How to record
- **One file per section**, named with the section code: `F1.m4a`, `M3.m4a` … (m4a, mp3 or wav;
  a phone voice-memo app is fine).
- Say the lines **in the order shown**, with **2 seconds of silence** before the first line and between lines.
  The files are cut automatically at the pauses, so don't talk or laugh in the gaps.
- **Mistake?** Stop, delete, and record that section again. They're short.
- Quiet room: switch off the fan, AC, TV and generator. Standing in front of a wardrobe full of clothes sounds great.
  Phone 20–30 cm from your mouth, a little to the side, in the same spot for the whole session.
- **Act it.** These are characters on a busy street at night, so speak up and play them big. Say each
  line in your own natural Pidgin; small wording changes are fine as long as the meaning stays the same.
  Where you see "...", leave a short dramatic pause (shorter than the 2 seconds between lines).
- One woman and one man can voice everyone. The game shifts each character's pitch slightly,
  but changing your voice a little for each named character helps a lot.
- Put every file in the `voices-raw/` folder in the project (no subfolders) and say which names to credit.

## Round 1: the first five minutes (every judge hears these)

### F1: Mama Nkechi (the tutorial customer, Papa’s oldest customer) (female)
_Older market woman, warm and motherly, a little teasing. She has known Aminu since he was small. Every player hears her first._

1. Ah, Aminu! Where your papa? ... Ehen. Your papa know.
2. Ehen! Na so your papa dey do am. God bless you, my son.
3. This one sweet well well! Mallam Sani go proud of you.
4. Aminu, my legs dey pain me o. Hurry small.
5. Aminu, you be my person now! I go tell the whole market.
6. My son! You be family now. Na only here I dey buy suya.
7. Ehen! You be Mallam Sani pikin true true. Oya, shake me!
8. Aminu! My daughter Adaeze dey marry next month. Na you go cater the owambe. Three hundred guests!

### F2: Everyday woman in the queue: hello (female)
_Any Lagos woman buying suya after work: nurse, aunty, tailor. Natural street Pidgin. Vary the energy a little from line to line. The order itself shows in the speech bubble, so just say the greeting._

1. Good evening o!
2. Oga, how far?
3. Abeg o!
4. Sannu o!
5. Evening boss.
6. Wetin dey happen? Abeg!
7. Na you dey here today? Okay.
8. Oga, abeg hurry up!
9. Ah! NEPA don take light again!

### F3: Everyday woman in the queue: happy / leaving (female)
_Happy and satisfied, except the last two: fed up of waiting and walking away._

1. Correct suya!
2. Oya, this one sweet die!
3. Thank you o! I go come back tomorrow.
4. E choke! Well done.
5. Na so! Your papa train you well.
6. Not bad o.
7. I don tire to wait. I dey go!
8. Na wa o. Make I find another suya spot.

### F4: Everyday woman in the queue: the Lagos handshake (female)
_A friendly regular offering a handshake, then laughing when the snap lands. Big and warm._

1. My guy! Shake me jare!
2. Oya, give me Lagos handshake!
3. Family! Bring hand!
4. Hehe! Correct guy!
5. Na so! You sabi am!
6. Eheh! Original Lagos handshake!
7. Hehe! Next time, snap am!
8. Ah, next time o!
9. You dey busy, I understand.

### M1: Chief Adebayo (the landlord): story (male)
_Rich landlord. Deep, slow, proud, a bit dramatic. "Hmph" is a disapproving grunt. His first line is the first voice in the game._

1. Your papa owe me rent. Three nights, Aminu. If this stall no make money, the Gbam Burger people go collect the space.
2. Na this one you call money? Gbam Burger people go pay me ten times this. Try again — or pack your load.
3. I don sign your new lease. One year. Rent every night, on time. Na business, Aminu.
4. Hmph... With this kind of crowd, Gbam Burger can wait. The space is yours, Aminu. But the rent — on time!
5. I hear say you sell well tonight. Hmph. Tomorrow I go come myself. We go see.
6. Their burger shop was empty tonight. Hmph. I knew this stall had something.

## Round 2: complaints, the regulars and the story endings

### F5: Everyday woman in the queue: complaints (meat) (female)
_Annoyed customer. Lagos-annoyed, not shouting-angry. The last two are said after a complaint._

1. Na wetin be this? No be wetin I order!
2. I talk one thing, you give me another thing!
3. Where the rest of my meat?
4. Na only this one? I order more than this!
5. I no order all this one o, but I no go pay for am.
6. This meat still dey moo! E never done!
7. Haba! Na raw meat you wan give me?
8. Na charcoal you wan sell me?!
9. This one don burn finish!
10. I dey pay half.
11. I no dey pay for this!

### M7: Mallam Sani (Aminu’s father) (male)
_Older man, just out of hospital: tired, gentle, proud of his son. Slow, with pauses where you see "..."._

1. The doctor say make I rest. So na you be the boss now. Grow this spot, my son. I paint new sign for you.
2. Three hundred sticks... My son, we go need a bigger fire.
3. I discharge myself to come see. ... Give me one stick. ... Aminu... you don use my pepper well. The spot na your own now.
4. Mama Nkechi call me. She say you dey do am like me. Keep the tin growing, my son.

### M8: Dayo (the quiet man in the black cap, secretly a food blogger) (male)
_Cool, few words. First two lines: barely impressed. The two long ones are his big reveal at the end of Act 1; the rest is Act 2, now relaxed and friendly._

1. Hmm.
2. ... Not bad.
3. I’m Dayo. People online know me as “Eat Lagos with Dayo”. I’ve been coming here three nights to see if the young man could keep his father’s standard. He did. My review goes up tonight.
4. I’m Dayo, “Eat Lagos with Dayo”. I came to see if the son could keep his father’s standard. ... Not yet. Maybe next time. But the street has spoken: this stall stays.
5. Dayo again. No disguise this time. My followers want to see the new spot.
6. This one go viral. Five stars.
7. Mallam Sani and Son... the standard is still there.
8. My phone battery dey die o, Aminu.
9. My followers love this spot.
10. Mallam Sani & Son — my number one suya in Lagos.
11. Lekki people dey ask me where Mallam Sani & Son dey. Gbam Burger no fit. Five stars — again.
